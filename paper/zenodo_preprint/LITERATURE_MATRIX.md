**Scope:** focused, targeted review for a single manuscript. This is **not** a
systematic review, meta-analysis or exhaustive prior-art search. The absence of a
study from this matrix is not evidence that no such study exists.

## 1. Search and verification method

Sources were identified from the RC1 novelty evidence, the project's Jakarta
literature, and targeted searches of Crossref and OpenAlex. Bibliographic
metadata were checked against DOI records, publisher pages, or Crossref/OpenAlex
metadata. Every entry in `input/References.bib` has a DOI or a stable official
URL; no placeholder or invented citation is used. The final manuscript cites 67
entries.

Verification status used below:

- **Metadata verified:** title, authors, year, venue and DOI checked against a
  DOI record or publisher page.
- **Full text read:** the full article was read for this draft.
- **Abstract/metadata only:** the argument was checked against the abstract and
  bibliographic record; full text was not available or not read.

Most entries are in the "abstract/metadata only" category. This is a limitation
of the positioning, not a claim that the papers say more than their abstracts.

## 4. What the literature does **not** support

The following claims are not made and should not be added without new evidence:

- "This is the first integrated heat–age–park framework." The closest prior work
  already combines these ingredients (Sidiqui et al., 2022; Kim & Kwak, 2026).
- "The combination of LST, age, OSM and MCDA is novel." Combination-only
  novelty fails.
- "M4 identifies age-vulnerable neighborhoods." The age term is a count, not a
  composition; the share scenario gives a different geography.
- "The top-priority cells lack parks." The observed pattern is a mechanical
  consequence of the additive park term and the observed input distribution.
- "The maps are Jakarta-wide." The common set is 2,536 of 2,765 cells and is
  selectively cooler/greener/more peripheral.
- "LISA proves significant clusters." The labels are convention-sensitive and
  not causal.
- "M4 improves health or policy outcomes." No outcome data or intervention
  evaluation is present.

## 5. Defensible contribution statement

> We provide an attribution and uncertainty audit of a Jakarta urban-heat
> screening index. We quantify how priority rankings and top-decile sets change
> when LST and vegetation are supplemented with population, an age count and a
> modelled candidate-park deficit; decompose the M3-to-M4 change into park,
> reweighting and age components; and show that an age count is nearly
> collinear with total population while an age share changes the objective. We
> also show that, under the observed inputs, the additive park term places the
> top-decile cutoff above the maximum score of all near-park cells — a property
> of the index, not evidence that parks are unimportant. The contribution is
> methodological (attribution/uncertainty), empirical (Jakarta) and
> reproducibility-oriented, not a claim of a new integrated framework or a
> validated vulnerability model.

## 6. Literature-search limitations

- Targeted rather than systematic; no PRISMA protocol, no exhaustive database
  string, no dual screening.
- Several papers were assessed from abstracts/metadata, not full texts.
- The search was performed on 20 November 2026; newer work may exist.
- Rahman et al. (2026) on Phoenix indicator sensitivity was mentioned in the RC1
  audit but its bibliographic metadata could not be verified in this round; it is
  therefore **not cited** in the manuscript. The same caution applies to any
  retrieval-limited lead.
- The matrix compares constructs and access specifications, not effect sizes;
  the studies use different samples, units and outcomes.
