## Description / Abstract

Urban heat screening indices combine land surface temperature, vegetation,
population and access to cooling space, but their maps depend on inclusion and
weighting choices. We examine how spatial priorities change across four
screening models in the five mainland administrative cities of Jakarta,
Indonesia, on a 500 m grid (2,536 common cells). M1 ranks land surface
temperature; M2 adds vegetation; M3 adds modelled total population; and M4 adds
a count of children and older adults plus walking-network distance to 1,015
OpenStreetMap candidate parks. Thermal and demographic inputs refer to a
declared 2024 period; the park and network snapshot is from November 2026. The
M1 and M4 top deciles share 141 of 254 cells (55.5% retention; Jaccard 0.384).
Between M3 and M4, a three-factor decomposition attributes 85.5% of absolute
rank movement to park addition, 13.2% to reweighting and 1.3% to the age-count
substitution. The age count is nearly collinear with population (Spearman
ρ = 0.9995): replacing it with total population leaves the top decile almost
unchanged (Jaccard 0.977), whereas an age-share specification changes it
substantially (0.347). Under the additive M4, the top-decile cutoff (0.8737)
exceeds the maximum score of all 537 near-park cells (0.8696), so no cell within
500 m of a candidate park enters the top 254; this is a property of the index
and observed inputs, not evidence that parks are unimportant. The 229 excluded
cells are cooler, greener and more peripheral than included cells, so the
results describe a conditional sample rather than all of Jakarta. The study is
an exploratory attribution and uncertainty analysis, not a validated
health-risk or public-access model.
