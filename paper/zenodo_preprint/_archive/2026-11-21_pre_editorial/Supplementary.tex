\section*{S5. Exclusion geography and score distributions}

Figure~\ref{fig:s1} maps the 229 excluded cells by primary exclusion reason.
Reasons overlap: a cell may be route-missing and also lack demographic support.
The primary reason is shown in the order low LST, population, age, route. The
route-missing category therefore contains most of the age-unsupported cells that
are also unroutable. Figure~\ref{fig:s2} shows the score distributions across
the common sample. Figure~\ref{fig:s3} shows the candidate-park inventory by
evidence state and the route-missing grid.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figures/figS2_exclusions.pdf}
  \caption{Excluded cells by primary reason. Route-missing cells dominate the
  exclusion pattern and are concentrated in the north and east. The map is a
  disclosure of sample selection, not a statement that excluded areas are
  unimportant. Source: \texttt{eligibility\_by\_model.csv} and the 500~m grid.}
  \label{fig:s1}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figures/figS1_score_distributions.pdf}
  \caption{Score distributions for M1--M4 on the common 2,536-cell sample.
  Percentile clipping compresses the upper tail and creates ties, especially in
  M1. The distributions are descriptive; they are not evidence that one model is
  more accurate than another.}
  \label{fig:s2}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figures/figS3_park_inventory.pdf}
  \caption{Candidate parks by evidence state (representative points) and
  route-missing grid cells. Grey points are unknown access, orange points are
  conditional/unverified, and green points carry a positive OSM tag that remains
  unverified. The map shows why the candidate inventory is a proxy, not a public
  park census.}

\end{figure}
